# Regi's Sentence Rain – the sentences, split into chunks by hand.
# scripts/build-satzregen.mjs turns this into src/data/satzregen/saetze.json.
#
# One line = one sentence:   chunk / chunk / chunk .  = note shown under the solved line  {flags}
#   Chunks are written as they stand inside the sentence ("io", "il sabato", "in un bar").
#   The first letter of slot 1 is capitalised in the display only.
#   *  finite verb (or auxiliary)      ^  subject
#   ~  other verb part (participle or infinitive after the auxiliary)
#   End: " ." or " ?" after the last chunk.
#   {alt 3 0 1 2}   another correct order, as chunk indices (several allowed)
#   {free 1<2 3-4}  every order of the chunks with these conditions, built into alts:
#                   1<2 = chunk 1 somewhere before chunk 2; 3-4 = chunk 3 right before chunk 4
#   {rule …}        own rule line for "These sentences were tricky"
#   {reg …}         regional note (content-plan §1.5), only where a learner will meet it in daily life
#
# Italian word order is freer than English, and the game must never reject a good sentence. What
# counts as correct: the neutral order and what speakers really say. Time and place can open the
# sentence, and with a noun subject the subject can follow the verb (In ospedale lavora mia
# sorella). A pronoun subject stays in front of the verb (io, tu, noi), and a verb at the very end
# ("Io caffè prendo") is not accepted: it can be heard for emphasis, but it is not what a learner
# should build. Questions: the question word stays in front of the verb; the subject may stand in
# front of the question word (Tu dove abiti?) or after the verb (Dove abiti tu?).
# Object pronouns (mi, ti, lo, la, ci, vi, li, le, gli, ne, me lo, glielo …) stand right before the
# verb, or before the auxiliary in the passato prossimo (mi ha chiamato), and the negation non stands in
# front of them.
# Italian drops subject pronouns; here they are written out in the chunks so that there is something
# to put in order. The notes say so where it matters.
# Modes keep their German ids: v2 = statements and wh-questions (A1), janein = yes/no questions
# (A1), klammer = object pronouns before the verb (A2), nebensatz = indirect questions (B1).
# Source: all sentences written for the game by the games run (2026-11-15), standard Italian (it-IT).
